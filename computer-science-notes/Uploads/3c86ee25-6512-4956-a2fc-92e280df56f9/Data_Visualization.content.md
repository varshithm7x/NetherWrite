What is Data Visualization?
Data visualization refers to the graphical representation of information and data using visual elements like charts, graphs, maps, and infographics. 
It transforms raw data into a visual format that is easier to understand and interpret.

Purpose: To simplify complex datasets, uncover insights, and communicate information effectively.

Examples: Bar charts for sales trends, heatmaps for website traffic, scatter plots for correlations between variables.
"The goal is to turn data into information and information into insight.” – Carly Fiorina


"Visualization gives you answers to questions you didn’t know you had." – Ben Shneiderman

## Slide 8

Types of Data Visualizations
Quantitative Data

• Numerical and measurable
• Includes discrete and continuous data
• Examples: Revenue, height, temperature
Qualitative Data

• Descriptive and categorical
• Includes nominal and ordinal data
• Examples: Colors, product categories, satisfaction level

## Slide 10

Principles of Good Visualization
ACCURACY
Ensure that visualizations represent data truthfully.

Avoid misleading scales or distortions that can misrepresent the data.​


CLARITY
Design visuals that are easy to understand at a glance.

Use clear labels, legends, and avoid unnecessary clutter.​



AESTHETICS
Use consistent color schemes and fonts to enhance readability.

Ensure that the design is visually appealing to maintain engagement.
​


EFFICIENCY
Present data in a way that allows viewers to quickly grasp insights.

Highlight key information without overwhelming details.​
